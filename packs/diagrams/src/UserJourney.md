## Use for
What a person does across the stages of an experience and how it feels at each one: onboarding, a purchase, a support case. The feeling curve is the point.

## Not for
- What the customer can see against what stays hidden: ServiceBlueprint.
- Steps with no feeling to report: Steps, or Timeline.
- Work by state: Kanban.
- Many personas at once: one UserJourney for each persona.

## Limits
- 6 stages, one JourneyStep for each. `check` warns above 6.
- Give every step a `score`, 1 (frustrated) to 5 (delighted). A stage with no score breaks the curve and the page reports it.
- The lowest stage is marked on its own. Give it a `pain`: what goes wrong there. One or two pains; not on every stage.
- `action` is a short phrase in the person's words; `touchpoint` is the surface (email, app, docs).
- Name the person with `persona`.

## Usual errors
- Stages in the wrong order. They draw left to right as listed.
- A score on every stage that reads 4. A flat curve says nothing: score what happened.
- Scores in other ranges (0 to 10). They are clamped to 1 to 5.
- Steps for two people. Make a second journey.
- A `pain` on every stage. Put it on the trough.
